Treetop Transitions? The Shifting Story of Bipedalism

By Wealthy Emeseh • December 30, 2025 • 4 min read

The evolutionary narrative has shifted once again. For decades, we were told that humans learned to walk upright as a response to moving out of the forests and onto the open savannah. But that theory has been quietly retired. The new claim? We began walking on two legs while still living in the trees.

This "new discovery" comes from observing chimpanzees in Tanzania who occasionally stand upright while navigating thin branches. From this fleeting behavior, researchers suggest our ancestors practiced bipedalism in the canopy before ever touching the ground. But a chimp balancing for a few seconds doesn’t make it a proto-human; it makes it a chimp.

“A chimp on two legs is not an ancestor; it is a chimp being a chimp.”

What is truly revealing is how often the "settled science" of evolution is rewritten. The fossils haven't changed. The data hasn't changed. Only the stories morph to keep the theory alive. Miocene fossils look like apes because they *are* apes. There is no biological need to invent imaginary transitional forms.

The biblical account remains the only consistent explanation. Scripture tells us man was created in God’s image—upright and fully human from the first breath. Apes were created separately, perfectly designed for their environments. No ambiguity, no missing links, and no need for a new story every ten years.
