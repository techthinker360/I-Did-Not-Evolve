Polonium Halos: Evidence for Earth’s Rapid Creation

By Wealthy Emeseh • December 13, 2025 • 5 min read

Decades ago, physicist Dr. Robert Gentry uncovered a remarkable feature within granite—the foundational rock of Earth’s crust. While examining these ancient stones, he discovered microscopic fossil-like rings known as radiohalos. These halos are the signature of polonium, a radioactive element that decays in mere minutes.

This finding presents a serious challenge to the conventional old-earth narrative. According to evolutionary geology, Earth began as a molten mass that cooled gradually over billions of years. But here’s the problem: polonium’s half-life is only about three minutes. If granite had taken thousands or millions of years to cool, any trace of polonium would have disappeared long before the rock solidified. Yet the halos remain—intact and unmistakable.

The presence of these short-lived isotopes frozen in solid granite suggests something profound: the rock must have formed rapidly, not slowly. This aligns far more closely with the biblical account of creation, where God spoke the Earth into existence in an instant, not over eons of time.
